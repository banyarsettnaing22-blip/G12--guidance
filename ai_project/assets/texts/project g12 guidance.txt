SUBJECT: BIOLOGY
============================================================

[CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION TO BIOLOGY]
============================================================

[TOPIC 1.1: THEMES OF BIOLOGY]
Biology is divided into five major themes: organization, information, 
energy and matter, interactions, and evolution.

Theme 1: New Properties Emerge at Successive Levels of Biological 
Organization - The hierarchy of life: biosphere, ecosystem, community, 
population, organism, organ system, organ, tissue, cell, organelle, 
molecule, atom. New properties emerge because of interactions among 
components at lower levels.

Theme 2: Life's Processes Involve the Expression and Transmission of 
Genetic Information - The genetic information is encoded in nucleotide 
sequences of DNA. DNA transmits heritable information from parents to 
offspring. DNA sequences (genes) act as instructions to make proteins 
through transcription (DNA to mRNA) and translation (mRNA to protein).

Theme 3: Life Requires the Transfer and Transformation of Energy and 
Matter - Energy flows through ecosystems. Producers convert energy from 
sunlight to chemical energy. Some energy is passed to consumers, and 
the rest is lost as heat.

Theme 4: Molecules to Ecosystems, Interactions in Biological Systems - 
Organisms interact continuously with physical factors. In negative 
feedback, accumulation of product slows its production. In positive 
feedback, product speeds up its own production.

Theme 5: Evolution for the Unity and Diversity of Life - Evolution 
is the process of change that has transformed life on Earth, accounting 
for the unity and diversity of life. Domain Bacteria and Archaea consist 
of prokaryotes. Domain Eukarya includes various groups of protists, 
fungi, plants, and animals.

[TOPIC 1.2: IMPORTANCE OF BIOLOGY IN DAILY LIFE]
Agriculture - Major agricultural products include foods, fibers, fuels, 
and raw materials. Myanmar's agricultural exports include rice, maize, 
blackgram, greengram, pigeonpea, chickpea, sesame, onion, tamarind, 
raw rubber, vegetables, and fruits.

Rice (Oryza sativa) production is based on environment resulting in 
rainfed lowland rice, winter rice, deep-water rice, upland rice, and 
irrigated rice. Wheat (Triticum aestivum) is a grass cultivated for 
its seeds. Maize (Zea mays) is a tropical plant that prefers warm 
humid weather.

Horticulture - Defined as the applied science of cultivating and 
growing plants for consumption and aesthetic purposes. Major types: 
olericulture (vegetable growing), pomology (fruits and nuts), 
viticulture (grapes), floriculture (flowers), turf management, 
arboriculture (trees), landscape horticulture, and postharvest 
physiology.

Hydroponic Culture - Growing plants without soil using water-based 
mineral nutrient solutions. Uses less water than traditional systems 
and allows faster growth and higher yields.

Aquaculture - Farming of aquatic organisms including fish, crustaceans, 
molluscs, algae, and aquatic plants. Fish farming (pisciculture) 
includes commercial breeding of carp, salmon, tilapia, and catfish. 
Mariculture involves cultivation of marine organisms.

Livestock - Domesticated land-living animals raised to provide meat, 
milk, eggs, feathers, transportation, or cultivation assistance. 
Common livestock: cattle, sheep, goats, pigs, horses, chickens, ducks.

Food - Food classification: heat/energy producing foods (carbohydrates 
and fats), body-building foods (protein), protective foods (vitamins 
and minerals), and luxury foods. Lactobacillus helps form curd from 
milk. Yeast is used in fermentation.

Health and Medicine - Pharmacognosy deals with secondary metabolites 
found in plants, animals, and microbial sources. Many modern medicines 
are derived from plants: salicylic acid (willow bark), quinine 
(cinchona tree), digitalis (foxglove), morphine and codeine (opium 
poppy).

Animal-based medicines include leeches (anti-coagulants for surgery), 
viper venom (bradykinin for hypertension treatment), maggots (wound 
debridement), spider silk (artificial ligaments), salmon calcitonin 
(bone loss treatment), and horseshoe crab blood (vaccine testing).

Clothing and Shelter - Plant fibres: cotton (Gossypium spp.), jute 
(Corchorus spp.). Sericulture involves rearing silkworm moths 
(Bombax mori) for silk production. Wool is obtained from sheep and 
other mammals. Wood: teak (Tectona grandis) and pyinkado 
(Xylia dolabriformis) are valuable timber yielding plants of Myanmar.

[CHAPTER 2: MOLECULAR BIOLOGY]
============================================================

[TOPIC 2.1: INTRODUCTION TO MOLECULAR BIOLOGY]
Molecular biology studies the molecular basis of biological activity 
in and between cells. Nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) are the main 
information-carrying molecules.

DNA Structure - Double helix consisting of two polynucleotide strands. 
Each nucleotide has: five-carbon deoxyribose sugar, phosphate group, 
and nitrogen-containing base. Bases: adenine (A), guanine (G) 
(purines, two rings), cytosine (C), thymine (T) (pyrimidines, 
single ring). Complementary base pairing: A pairs with T (2 hydrogen 
bonds), G pairs with C (3 hydrogen bonds). Strands are antiparallel 
(5' to 3' and 3' to 5').

RNA Structure - Single-stranded, contains adenine (A), uracil (U), 
cytosine (C), and guanine (G). Types: mRNA (template for translation), 
tRNA (carries amino acids), rRNA (reads codon on mRNA).

DNA Replication - Semiconservative method. Helicase unwinds the 
double helix. DNA polymerase III cannot initiate synthesis; needs 
RNA primer from primase. Leading strand forms continuously toward 
replication fork. Lagging strand forms in Okazaki fragments (100-200 
nucleotides) joined by DNA ligase. DNA polymerase I carries out 
mismatch repair. Telomeres protect chromosome ends.

[TOPIC 2.2: GENETIC CODE]
Genetic code is a set of rules converting nucleotide sequence to amino 
acid sequence. Triplet code produces 64 possible combinations (4×4×4). 
AUG is start codon coding for methionine. UAA, UAG, UGA are 
terminator codons.

Characteristics: (1) Redundant - multiple codons can code for same 
amino acid. (2) Continuous - reads as series of three-letter codons 
without spaces. (3) Nearly universal - same codon codes for same amino 
acid in almost all organisms.

[TOPIC 2.3: PROTEIN SYNTHESIS]
Transcription - Occurs in nucleus. mRNA synthesized based on DNA 
template. Stages: initiation (RNA polymerase binds), elongation 
(mRNA strand synthesized 5' to 3'), termination (RNA polymerase 
detaches at termination signal). Pre-mRNA modified: 5' cap added, 
3' poly-A tail added, introns removed by splicing.

Translation - Occurs in cytoplasm. Converts nucleotide sequence to 
amino acid sequence. Ribosome has small subunit (mRNA attachment) and 
large subunit (A, P, E sites for tRNA). tRNA has anticodon and carries 
specific amino acid.

Initiation: Small ribosomal subunit attaches near start codon (AUG), 
initiator tRNA with UAC anticodon binds, large subunit joins.

Elongation: tRNA with polypeptide in P site, peptide bond forms, 
tRNA with next amino acid enters A site, polypeptide chain transferred, 
mRNA moves forward by one codon.

Termination: Stop codon reached, release factor cleaves polypeptide 
from last tRNA.

[TOPIC 2.4: GENETIC ENGINEERING]
Genetic engineering - Alteration of genetic material. Transgenic 
organisms (GMOs) have foreign DNA introduced to their genome.

Recombinant DNA - Transferring gene from one organism to another 
using vector (plasmid, virus, liposome, or gene gun).

Golden Rice - Genetically modified rice containing beta-carotene 
(converted to vitamin A). Benefits: increased crop yield, heat and 
drought tolerance, pest resistance, enhanced nutritional value.

Genetically Modified Animals - Foreign gene inserted into animal oocyte 
which is fertilized and implanted in host female. Used to produce 
pharmaceutical products (human proteins in milk).

[CHAPTER 3: TRANSPORT SYSTEM IN ORGANISMS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 3.1: TRANSPORT SYSTEM IN PLANTS]
Xylem transports water and inorganic ions from roots. Phloem transports 
sucrose and amino acids from leaves to other parts. Xylem vessels are 
dead, empty hollow cells with no end walls. Phloem sieve tubes are 
living cells with cytoplasm but no nucleus.

[TOPIC 3.2: TRANSPORT MECHANISMS IN PLANTS]
Apoplastic pathway - Water moves through cell walls, dead cells, and 
xylem tubes. Occurs more rapidly. Casparian strip (suberin band) 
blocks apoplastic pathway at endodermis.

Symplastic pathway - Water moves through cytoplasm and plasmodesmata 
or vacuole. Slower than apoplastic pathway.

[TOPIC 3.3: TRANSPIRATION]
Transpiration - Evaporation of water from leaves through stomata. 
Factors affecting rate: humidity (lower humidity = faster), wind speed 
(faster wind = faster), temperature (higher temperature = faster), 
light intensity (stomata open during day), very dry conditions 
(plants may close stomata).

Benefits: ascent of sap, removal of excess water, cooling effect 
(10-15°C reduction), mechanical tissue development, mineral salt 
distribution, increasing mineral salt concentration, root system 
development, quality of fruits, resistance to moderate drought, 
turgidity, supplies water for photosynthesis.

[TOPIC 3.4: TRANSLOCATION]
Translocation - Movement of dissolved substances through plant. 
Food synthesized in leaves passes downwards or upwards in sieve tubes 
of phloem. Both upward and downward movement may occur simultaneously.

[TOPIC 3.5: TRANSPORT SYSTEM IN ANIMALS]
Mass transport system features: exchange surfaces, system of vessels, 
way to ensure correct direction, way to move materials fast enough, 
suitable transport medium, way to adapt rate of transport.

Open circulatory system - Blood pumped by tubular heart leaves 
open-ended vessels to blood spaces surrounding tissues. Found in 
arthropods (insects, spiders, crabs).

Closed circulatory system - Blood pumped by heart into blood vessels. 
Single circulation (fish): blood passes once through heart. Double 
circulation (birds and mammals): blood passes twice through heart.

[TOPIC 3.6: CIRCULATORY SYSTEM]
Systemic circulation carries oxygenated blood from heart to body cells. 
Pulmonary circulation carries deoxygenated blood from heart to lungs.

[TOPIC 3.7: CARDIOVASCULAR SYSTEM IN MAMMALS]
Heart structure - Four chambers: right atrium, left atrium, right 
ventricle, left ventricle. Tricuspid valve (right), bicuspid/mitral 
valve (left). Semilunar valves at base of pulmonary artery and aorta.

Blood vessels - Arteries carry blood away from heart (thick walls with 
elastic fibers). Veins carry blood back to heart (thinner walls, 
semilunar valves). Capillaries have very thin walls (one cell thick) 
for diffusion.

Blood components - Plasma (50% of blood volume), erythrocytes 
(red blood cells, 5 million per mm³, contain haemoglobin, no nucleus), 
leucocytes (white blood cells, 4,000-11,000 per mm³, defend against 
infection), platelets (150,000-400,000 per mm³, involved in blood 
clotting).

Blood clotting process - Thromboplastin converts prothrombin to 
thrombin. Thrombin converts fibrinogen to fibrin. Fibrin forms mesh 
covering wound and trapping red blood cells.

Cardiac cycle - Atrial systole (contraction of atria), ventricular 
systole (contraction of ventricles), diastole (relaxation). One cycle 
lasts about 0.8 seconds in humans.

Control of heartbeat - Sinoatrial node (SAN) is natural pacemaker 
in right atrium. Excitation spreads to atrioventricular node (AVN), 
then bundle of His, then Purkyne tissue.

[CHAPTER 4: DISEASES IN PLANTS AND ANIMALS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 4.1: PLANT DISEASES]
Plant disease - Abnormal growth and/or dysfunction of plant. 
Biotic diseases caused by living organisms (fungi, bacteria, viruses). 
Abiotic diseases caused by non-living environmental conditions.

Disease triangle - Three factors: host plant, pathogen, environmental 
conditions.

Fungal diseases - Over 8,000 species. Genera: Alternaria (leaf spots), 
Aspergillus (rots of seeds), Botrytis (blight), Fusarium (root rot), 
Puccinia (rust of cereals), Rhizopus (soft rot of fruits), 
Sclerotinia (soft rot of vegetables).

Bacterial diseases - Approximately 200 types. Genera: Agrobacterium 
(crown gall), Erwinia (fire blight), Pseudomonas (leaf spot), 
Streptomyces (scab of potato), Xanthomonas (blight of beans, rice).

Viral diseases - Intracellular pathogenic particles. Examples: tobacco 
mosaic, tomato mosaic, barley yellow dwarf, potato leaf roll.

Control methods - Biological (living microorganisms), physical 
(temperature, moisture, irradiation), chemical (bactericides, 
fungicides, nematicides).

[TOPIC 4.2: ANIMAL DISEASES]
Non-infectious diseases - Not caused by pathogens. Causes: aging, 
genetics, malnutrition, lifestyle, environment. Examples: cardiovascular 
disease, cancer, diabetes, Alzheimer's.

Cardiovascular disease - Atheroma formation: white blood cells and 
lipids clump under endothelium forming fatty streaks. Atherosclerosis 
is hardening of arteries.

Angina: Plaques build in coronary arteries causing chest pain during 
exercise. Myocardial infarction (heart attack): coronary artery 
completely blocked by blood clot. Stroke: interruption of normal blood 
supply to brain. Deep vein thrombosis (DVT): blood clot in deep veins.

Risk factors - Non-modifiable: genes, age, gender. Modifiable: smoking, 
inactivity, high blood pressure, diet and obesity.

Infectious diseases - Caused by pathogens. Malaria: caused by four 
species of Plasmodium. Transmitted by female Anopheles mosquito. 
Plasmodium multiplies in human liver and red blood cells. Treatment: 
quinine, chloroquine, proguanil, mefloquine, artesunate. Prevention: 
reduce mosquitoes, avoid being bitten, use drugs.

[CHAPTER 5: COORDINATION AND RESPONSE]
============================================================

[TOPIC 5.1: COORDINATION AND RESPONSE IN PLANTS]
Stimulus - Detectable change in internal or external environment. 
Plant hormones (phytohormones) coordinate plant responses.

[TOPIC 5.2: PLANT GROWTH REGULATORS]
Auxin - First plant growth regulator. Indole acetic acid (IAA). 
Effects: root inhibition, parthenocarpy (seedless fruits), cell 
elongation, abscission, cell division.

Gibberellin - Produced in young organs. Effects: fruit growth, 
stem and leaf growth, seed germination (stimulates hydrolytic 
enzymes like amylases).

Cytokinin - Adenine derivative. Effects: flowering, promotes lateral 
bud development, development of chloroplast.

Ethylene - Only gaseous phytohormone. Effects: stimulates fruit 
ripening, causes drooping of leaves and flowers, induces rapid growth 
of internodes.

Abscisic acid - Stress hormone. Effects: causes abscission, includes 
synthesis of carotenoids, acts as antagonist of all growth promoters.

Seed dormancy - Internal inhibition of germination. Causes: hard seed 
coats, immature embryo, light sensitivity, germination inhibitors. 
Broken by scarification, stratification, hormonal treatment.

Seed germination types - Epigeal (cotyledons pushed above ground, 
e.g., bean), hypogeal (cotyledons stay in soil, e.g., mango, coconut), 
vivipary (germination inside fruit, e.g., mangroves).

Photoperiodism - Effect of day length on flowering. Short day plants 
(soybean, onion), long day plants (radish, potato), day neutral plants 
(tomato, cotton).

[TOPIC 5.3: COORDINATION AND RESPONSE IN ANIMALS]
Human nervous system - Central nervous system (brain and spinal cord), 
peripheral nervous system.

Brain parts - Cerebrum (largest, intellect, learning, memory), 
thalamus (relay station), hypothalamus (regulates internal environment), 
midbrain (visual and auditory processing), cerebellum (coordination of 
posture and movements), pons (relay centre), medulla oblongata 
(reflexes, heart rate, breathing).

Neurones - Dendrites receive impulses, axons conduct impulses away 
from cell body. Myelin sheath insulates and increases speed.

Synapses - Connection between neurones. Neurotransmitters carry 
signal across synaptic cleft. Acetylcholine, dopamine, serotonin, 
endorphins, norepinephrine.

[TOPIC 5.4: SENSE ORGANS]
Eye structure - Sclera (outer coat), cornea (transparent), iris 
(controls pupil size), lens (focuses light), retina (contains rod 
cells for night vision and cone cells for colour vision), fovea 
(sharper vision), optic nerve (transmits impulses).

Accommodation - Focusing on distant objects: ciliary muscles relax, 
lens flattens. Focusing on near objects: ciliary muscles contract, 
lens becomes more convex.

[TOPIC 5.5: ENDOCRINE SYSTEM]
Endocrine glands - Anterior pituitary (hGH, TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, 
prolactin), posterior pituitary (ADH, oxytocin), thyroid (thyroxine, 
calcitonin), parathyroid (PTH), adrenal cortex (glucocorticoids, 
mineralocorticoids), adrenal medulla (epinephrine, norepinephrine), 
pancreas (insulin, glucagon), ovaries (estrogen, progesterone), 
testes (testosterone).

[TOPIC 6.1: BIODIVERSITY]
Three levels - Ecosystem diversity (variation in ecosystems), species 
diversity (number of species), genetic diversity (genetic variation 
within species).

[TOPIC 6.2: BIODIVERSITY IN MYANMAR]
Forest types - Mixed deciduous (38%), Hill and temperate evergreen 
(25%), Tropical evergreen (16%), Dry forest (10%), Deciduous 
dipterocarp (5%), Tidal/beach/dune/swamp (4%), Fallow land (2%).

Endangered species - Asian elephant (Elephas maximus), Eld's deer 
(Rucervus eldii thamin), Irrawaddy dolphin (Orcaella brevirostris), 
green peafowl (Pavo muticus), Burmese star tortoise (Geochelone 
platynota).

[TOPIC 6.3: THREATS TO BIODIVERSITY]
Major threats - Habitat destruction, climate change, pollution, 
overexploitation, invasive alien species.

Habitat destruction causes: large-scale industry, mining, cattle 
rearing, commercial fishing, dam construction, plantation cropping, 
agriculture.

Invasive alien species - Water hyacinth, golden apple snails.

Genetic pollution - Transfer of genes from crop plants to wild 
species. Related to GMO contamination.

Overexploitation - Overfishing, overlogging, overhunting. Illegal 
wildlife trade valued at $10 billion per year.

Climate change - Rising ocean temperatures, diminishing Arctic sea ice, 
shifts in vegetation zones.

Human overpopulation - Strains on resources leading to deforestation, 
decreased biodiversity, pollution, and emissions.

[TOPIC 6.4: CONSERVATION]
In situ conservation - Natural habitat (wildlife sanctuaries, national 
parks, biosphere reserves).

Ex situ conservation - Outside natural habitats: captive breeding, 
seed banks, botanical gardens.

Protected areas in Myanmar - Scientific reserve, national park, marine 
national park, nature reserve, wildlife sanctuary, community forest.

SUBJECT: CHEMISTRY
============================================================

[CHAPTER 1: CHEMICAL BONDING AND INTERMOLECULAR FORCES]
============================================================

[TOPIC 1.1: BASIC CONCEPTS]
Valence electrons - Electrons in outermost shell. Equal to group 
number in Periodic Table. Only valence electrons can take part in 
chemical bonding. Octet rule: atoms gain, lose, or share electrons 
to achieve stable electronic structure of noble gases (8 electrons 
in valence shell).

Electronegativity (EN) - Measure of ability of atom to attract 
shared electrons. Electron affinity (EA) - Energy released when 
electron added to gaseous atom. Ionisation energy (IE) - Energy 
required to remove electron from gaseous atom.

Types of chemical bonding - Ionic bonding, covalent bonding, metallic 
bonding. Intermolecular forces: hydrogen bonding, van der Waals 
forces (ion-dipole, dipole-dipole, London dispersion forces).

[TOPIC 1.2: IONIC BONDING]
Ionic bonding - Transfer of electrons from atom with small ionisation 
energy to atom with large electron affinity. Electrostatic attractions 
between cations and anions. Occurs when electronegativity difference 
> 1.8.

Crystal lattice - Geometric arrangement of ions. Unit cell is smallest 
repeating unit. Simple cubic (sc), face-centred cubic (fcc), 
body-centred cubic (bcc). In NaCl, coordination ratio is 6:6.

[TOPIC 1.3: COVALENT BONDING]
Covalent bonding - Sharing one or more pairs of valence electrons 
between atoms. Occurs between non-metals. Single bonds (one pair), 
double bonds (two pairs), triple bonds (three pairs). Higher number 
of bonded electrons = shorter bond length, stronger bond.

Coordinate bond (dative bond) - Both shared electrons donated by one 
atom. Example: NH₄⁺, H₃O⁺.

Lewis structures - Steps: (1) Calculate total valence electrons, 
(2) Write skeleton arrangement, (3) Subtract shared electrons from 
total, place remaining as unshared pairs, (4) Insert lone pairs to 
complete octet.

Exceptions to octet rule - Electron-deficient molecules (NO, BeCl₂, 
BF₃), expanded valence shell molecules (PF₅, ICl₃, SF₆).

VSEPR theory - Valence Shell Electron Pair Repulsion: electron pairs 
arrange to minimise repulsion. Repulsion order: bonding pair-bonding 
pair < lone pair-bonding pair < lone pair-lone pair.

Shapes - Linear (AX₂, 180°), triangular planar (AX₃, 120°), 
tetrahedral (AX₄, 109.5°).

[TOPIC 1.4: INTERMOLECULAR FORCES]
Polar molecules - Difference in electronegativity between bonded 
atoms creates dipole. Non-polar molecules have equal distribution of 
charge.

Dipole-dipole interaction - Between two dipolar molecules.
Ion-dipole interaction - Between ion and neutral molecule with dipole.
London dispersion forces - Temporary dipoles from unsymmetrical 
electron distribution. Weakest intermolecular forces, exist in all 
substances.
Hydrogen bonding - H bonded to electronegative atom (F, O, or N) 
interacting with lone pair on another electronegative atom. Strongest 
intermolecular force.

[TOPIC 1.5: METALLIC BONDING]
Metallic bond - Valence electrons form "sea of electrons" around 
positive metallic nuclei. Strength increases with: increasing positive 
charge on ions, decreasing size of metal ions, increasing number of 
mobile electrons.

[CHAPTER 2: ENERGY CHANGES IN CHEMICAL REACTIONS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 2.1: ENERGY CHANGES]
Chemical energy - Energy stored in bonds of chemical compounds. 
Energy changes during reactions observed as temperature changes or 
light emission.

Spontaneous reactions - Occur without addition of energy. 
Non-spontaneous reactions - Need energy to initiate.

Exothermic reactions - Give out energy (products have lower energy 
than reactants). Examples: combustion, neutralisation, respiration, 
rusting.

Endothermic reactions - Absorb energy (products have higher energy 
than reactants). Examples: melting ice, cooking egg, thermal 
decomposition.

[TOPIC 2.2: ENTHALPY CHANGES]
Enthalpy change (ΔH) - Heat absorbed or released at constant pressure. 
Positive for endothermic, negative for exothermic.

Standard enthalpy change (ΔH°) - At standard conditions: 25°C (298K), 
1 atm pressure.

Thermochemical equation - Balanced equation including physical states 
and ΔH.

Measurement - Calorimeter. q = mcΔT. Specific heat capacity of 
water = 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹.

[TOPIC 2.3: HESS'S LAW]
Hess's Law - Enthalpy change for reaction is same regardless of route 
taken. Can be used to calculate enthalpy changes that cannot be 
measured directly.

Enthalpy cycle - Constructed using formation or combustion data.

Bond energy (bond enthalpy) - Energy needed to break one mole of 
chemical bonds. Bond breaking absorbs energy (positive ΔH), bond 
forming releases energy (negative ΔH).

[CHAPTER 3: CHEMICAL KINETICS: RATES OF REACTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 3.1: REACTION RATES]
Rate = change in concentration of reactants or products per unit time.
Rate = -Δ[reactant]/Δt = +Δ[product]/Δt.

[TOPIC 3.2: COLLISION THEORY AND ACTIVATION ENERGY]
Collision theory - Reactant particles must collide in correct 
orientation with sufficient energy to overcome energy barrier.

Activation energy (Ea) - Minimum energy required for successful 
collision. Activated complex - temporary unstable intermediate.

[TOPIC 3.3: FACTORS AFFECTING REACTION RATES]
Concentration - Higher concentration = more collisions = faster rate.
Particle size - Smaller particles = larger surface area = faster rate.
Pressure - Higher pressure on gases = faster rate.
Temperature - Higher temperature = more kinetic energy = more 
successful collisions = faster rate. For every 10°C rise, rate 
approximately doubles.

Catalysts - Alter rate without being consumed. Positive catalysts 
increase rate, negative catalysts decrease rate. Homogeneous catalysts 
same phase as reactants. Heterogeneous catalysts different phase.

Light - Photochemical reactions initiated by light. Brighter light = 
faster reaction.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 4: CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM]
============================================================

[TOPIC 4.1: STATE OF DYNAMIC EQUILIBRIUM]
Reversible reactions - Can proceed in both forward and reverse 
directions. Dynamic equilibrium: forward and reverse reactions occur 
at equal rates, concentrations remain constant.

[TOPIC 4.2: LE CHATELIER'S PRINCIPLE]
Le Chatelier's Principle - If system at equilibrium is subjected to 
change, equilibrium shifts to counteract the change.

Effect of concentration - Adding reactant shifts equilibrium right 
(more products). Adding product shifts equilibrium left.

Effect of temperature - Temperature increase favours endothermic 
reaction. Temperature decrease favours exothermic reaction.

Effect of pressure - Pressure increase shifts equilibrium toward 
side with fewer moles of gas. Pressure decrease shifts toward side 
with more moles of gas.

[TOPIC 4.3: EQUILIBRIUM CONSTANTS]
Law of mass action - For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, 
K_eq = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b.

K_c uses concentration, K_p uses partial pressures. Large K_eq (>1) 
= more products. Small K_eq (<1) = more reactants.

[CHAPTER 5: ACID-BASE REACTIONS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 5.1: THEORIES OF ACIDS AND BASES]
Arrhenius theory - Acids produce H⁺ ions in water. Bases produce 
OH⁻ ions in water.

Brønsted-Lowry theory - Acids are proton donors. Bases are proton 
acceptors. Conjugate acid-base pair: acid and its conjugate base or 
base and its conjugate acid.

Lewis theory - Acids accept electron pairs. Bases donate electron 
pairs.

[TOPIC 5.2: IONIC DISSOCIATION OF WATER AND pH]
Ionic product of water - K_w = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25°C.
pH = -log[H⁺], pOH = -log[OH⁻], pH + pOH = 14.

[TOPIC 5.3: IONISATION OF ACIDS AND BASES]
Strong acids - Completely ionise in water (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄).
Weak acids - Partially ionise (HF, CH₃COOH).
Strong bases - Completely ionise (NaOH, KOH).
Weak bases - Partially ionise (NH₃).

Acid dissociation constant (K_a) = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]. Larger K_a = 
stronger acid. Base dissociation constant (K_b) = [BH⁺][OH⁻]/[B].

[TOPIC 5.4: SALT HYDROLYSIS]
Salts of strong acid and strong base - Neutral (NaCl, KNO₃).
Salts of weak acid and strong base - Basic (CH₃COONa).
Salts of strong acid and weak base - Acidic (NH₄Cl).
Salts of weak acid and weak base - Depends on relative strengths.

[TOPIC 5.5: BUFFER SOLUTIONS]
Buffer solution - Resists pH changes on dilution or addition of small 
amounts of acid or base. Types: weak acid and its salt (CH₃COOH/
CH₃COONa), weak base and its salt (NH₃/NH₄Cl).

[CHAPTER 6: TRANSITION ELEMENTS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 6.1: THE 3d SERIES TRANSITION ELEMENTS]
Transition elements - Atoms with partially filled d subshells or can 
give rise to cations with incomplete d subshells. 3d series: Sc to Zn.

Electronic configuration - 4s orbital filled before 3d. Exceptions: 
Cr ([Ar]4s¹3d⁵), Cu ([Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰).

[TOPIC 6.2: CHARACTERISTIC PROPERTIES]
Physical properties - Solid metals, malleable, ductile, metallic lustre, 
high tensile strength, good conductors, high melting and boiling points.

Variable oxidation states - Multiple oxidation states due to removal 
of s and d electrons. Most stable: +3 at beginning, +2 at end.

Coloured compounds and ions - Due to incomplete d orbitals. Examples: 
Ti³⁺ purple, V³⁺ green, Cr³⁺ violet, Mn²⁺ pale pink, Fe²⁺ green, 
Co²⁺ pink, Ni²⁺ green, Cu²⁺ blue.

Magnetic properties - Paramagnetic (unpaired electrons), diamagnetic 
(paired electrons), ferromagnetic (strongly attracted, retains 
magnetism).

Catalytic properties - Iron (Haber process), vanadium(V) oxide 
(Contact process), nickel (hydrogenation).

[CHAPTER 7: CHEMISTRY AND GREEN ENVIRONMENT]
============================================================

[TOPIC 7.1: OUR ENVIRONMENTAL WORLD]
Carbon cycle - Photosynthesis, respiration, exchange, fossilisation, 
sedimentation, combustion.

Nitrogen cycle - Nitrogen fixation, nitrification, nitrogen 
assimilation, ammonification, denitrification.

Phosphorus cycle - No gaseous phase. Cycles through water, soil, 
sediments. Marine birds return phosphorus from ocean to land through 
guano.

Sulphur cycle - Moves through rocks, water bodies, atmosphere, living 
systems. Sources: volcanic eruptions, decaying organisms, industrial 
processes.

[TOPIC 7.2: POLLUTANTS AND IMPACTS]
Heavy metals - Arsenic (As³⁺, As⁵⁺), cadmium (Cd²⁺), lead (Pb²⁺, 
Pb⁴⁺), mercury (Hg⁰, Hg⁺, Hg²⁺). Bioaccumulation of methylmercury 
in fish.

Pesticides - Organophosphates, organochlorines, carbamates, 
thiocarbamates, pyrethroids.

POPs - Persistent Organic Pollutants (PCBs, DDT). Long-lasting, 
lipophilic, accumulate in fatty tissue.

VOCs - Volatile Organic Compounds. Contribute to ozone and fine 
particulates formation, smog.

[TOPIC 7.3: RADIOACTIVE SUBSTANCES]
Radioactivity - Spontaneous decay of heavy nuclei emitting alpha, 
beta, gamma radiation. Alpha particles blocked by paper, beta by 
aluminium foil, gamma by concrete or lead.

Radioisotopes uses - Carbon-14 (radiocarbon dating), Uranium-235 
(nuclear fuel), Cesium-137 (medical therapy).

[TOPIC 7.4: GREEN TECHNIQUES]
Biofuels - Bioethanol from sugar cane, corn, lignocellulosic materials. 
Biodiesel from plant oils (remove glycerine).

Wastewater treatment - Agricultural waste adsorbents, activated carbon, 
rice husk ceramics, photocatalysts (TiO₂, ZnO, Fe₂O₃).

Green synthesis - Adipic acid from glucose. Trivalent chromium 
plating (less toxic than hexavalent chromium).

============================================================
[CHAPTER 8: ORGANIC COMPOUNDS AND MACROMOLECULES]
============================================================

[TOPIC 8.1: ORGANIC COMPOUNDS]
Ethers - R-O-R'. Dehydration of alcohols with H₂SO₄ at 140°C.

Aldehydes and Ketones - RCHO and RCOR'. Oxidation of primary alcohols 
gives aldehydes, secondary alcohols gives ketones. Hydration of 
alkynes gives aldehydes (ethyne) or ketones (other alkynes).

Carboxylic acids - RCOOH. Reaction with metals (salt + H₂), bases 
(salt + H₂O), carbonates (salt + CO₂ + H₂O). Reduction with LiAlH₄ 
gives primary alcohols.

Esters - RCOOR'. Esterification: carboxylic acid + alcohol with 
H₂SO₄. Acidic hydrolysis gives carboxylic acid and alcohol. Basic 
hydrolysis (saponification) gives sodium salt and alcohol.

Amines - RNH₂ (primary), R₂NH (secondary), R₃N (tertiary). Prepared 
by heating haloalkane with excess ammonia.

Amides - RCONH₂. Prepared by condensation of carboxylic acid with 
ammonia.

[TOPIC 8.2: FUNCTIONAL GROUPS]
Chemical tests - 2,4-DNP (orange precipitate for aldehydes/ketones), 
Fehling's test (reddish-brown precipitate for aldehydes).

Infrared spectroscopy - Detects bond vibrations. Characteristic 
absorptions: C-O (1050-1410 cm⁻¹), C=C (1620-1680), C=O (1650-1750), 
O-H (2500-3300), C-H (2850-3090), N-H (3300-3500).

[TOPIC 8.3: MACROMOLECULES]
Addition polymers - Monomers with C=C bond. Examples: polyethene 
(PET), polyvinyl chloride (PVC), Teflon, polystyrene.

Condensation polymers - Two different functional groups, eliminate 
small molecule (H₂O). Polyesters (terylene/PET from terephthalic 
acid and ethylene glycol). Polyamides (nylon 6,6 from adipic acid 
and 1,6-diaminohexane).

Proteins - Polyamides made from amino acids. Peptide bond (amide 
link) between NH₂ of one amino acid and COOH of another.

Biodegradable plastics - Contain starch granules or made from plant 
materials (polylactic acid, PLA). Photodegradable plastics contain 
carbonyl groups that absorb UV light to weaken and break down.

SUBJECT: PHYSICS
============================================================

[CHAPTER 1: ROTATIONAL MOTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 1.1: ROTATIONAL MOTION UNDER CONSTANT ANGULAR ACCELERATION]
Kinematic equations for angular motion: ω = ω₀ + αt, 
θ = ω₀t + ½αt², ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ.

[TOPIC 1.2: RELATIONS BETWEEN LINEAR AND ANGULAR QUANTITIES]
s = rθ, v = rω, a_T = rα (tangential acceleration).

[TOPIC 1.3: CENTRIPETAL ACCELERATION]
Centripetal acceleration: a_c = v²/r = rω². Direction: toward centre 
of circle. Resultant acceleration in nonuniform circular motion: 
a = √(a_c² + a_T²), tanφ = a_T/a_c.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 2: FORCES IN CIRCULAR MOTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 2.1: FORCES CAUSING CENTRIPETAL ACCELERATION]
Centripetal force: F_C = mv²/r = mrω². Banking angle: tanθ = v²/rg.

[TOPIC 2.2: CENTRIPETAL AND CENTRIFUGAL FORCES]
Centripetal force is real force toward centre. Centrifugal force is 
apparent force away from centre. Applications: centrifuge, washing 
machine, centrifugal pump, centrifugal governor.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 3: FLUID DYNAMICS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 3.1: LAMINAR AND TURBULENT FLOW]
Laminar flow - Smooth paths in layers, no mixing. Turbulent flow - 
Irregular paths, whirling, direction constantly changing.

[TOPIC 3.2: EQUATION OF CONTINUITY]
A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ (volume flow rate constant for incompressible fluid).

[TOPIC 3.3: BERNOULLI'S EQUATION]
p + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant. Sum of pressure energy, kinetic energy 
per unit volume, potential energy per unit volume remains constant.

Manifestations: lift on aeroplane wings, insect sprayer, Magnus effect 
on spinning ball, roof flying away during cyclones.

[TOPIC 3.4: VISCOSITY]
Viscosity - Internal resistance of fluid to motion. Newton's law: 
τ = -η(dv/dy). SI unit: pascal second (Pa·s) or poiseuille (Pl).

Stokes' law: F_drag = 6πηrv (for spherical object in viscous fluid). 
Terminal velocity: v_t = 2r²(ρ_particle - ρ_fluid)g/9η.

[TOPIC 3.5: SURFACE TENSION AND CAPILLARITY]
Surface tension: γ = F/l (force per unit length). SI unit: N/m.
Capillary rise: h = 2γcosθ/ρgr.

[CHAPTER 4: SOURCES OF ENERGY]
============================================================

[TOPIC 4.1: ENERGY DEMAND AND CRISIS]
Non-renewable resources - Limited supply, not replenished at rate 
consumed (fossil fuels, nuclear energy).

Renewable resources - Replenished by natural processes (solar, wind, 
hydro, biomass, geothermal).

[TOPIC 4.2: SOLAR ENERGY]
Solar constant - 1350 W/m² at top of atmosphere. Photovoltaic cells 
convert light to electricity. Concentrated solar power uses mirrors 
to focus sunlight.

[TOPIC 4.3: WIND ENERGY]
Wind power: P = ½ρAv³. Wind turbine converts kinetic energy to 
electrical energy.

[TOPIC 4.4: HYDROPOWER]
Available power: P = ηρV·gh. Impoundment facilities use dams. 
Run-of-river uses natural decline. Pumped storage stores energy.

[TOPIC 4.5: OCEAN ENERGY]
Tidal energy - From difference in sea levels. Wave energy - From 
kinetic energy of waves. Ocean thermal energy - From temperature 
difference between surface and deep water (minimum 20°C).

[TOPIC 4.6: GEOTHERMAL ENERGY]
Energy from Earth's core. Limited to regions with hot underground 
rocks. Pumps water into heated rocks, steam drives turbines.

[TOPIC 4.7: BIOMASS]
Biomass sources: wood, agricultural crops, animal manure, municipal 
solid waste. Conversion methods: direct combustion, thermochemical 
(pyrolysis, gasification), chemical (transesterification), biological 
(fermentation, anaerobic digestion).

Environmental impacts - Global warming (greenhouse gases: CO₂, CH₄, 
NOx, CFCs), acid rain (SO₂, NOx), air pollution, water pollution.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 5: HEAT TRANSFER AND THERMODYNAMICS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 5.1: HEAT TRANSFER]
Conduction - Heat transfer by adjacent parts without bulk motion. 
H_cond = kA(T₂-T₁)/l. Thermal conductivity (k) measures ability to 
conduct heat.

Convection - Heat transfer by fluid motion. Natural convection driven 
by buoyant force. Forced convection from external means.

Radiation - Heat transfer through electromagnetic waves without medium. 
Stefan-Boltzmann law: ε = σT⁴. Emissivity (e) = ratio of object's 
emissive power to blackbody's. H_rad = eσAT⁴.

[TOPIC 5.2: THERMODYNAMICS]
Zeroth law - Two objects in thermal equilibrium with third object are 
in equilibrium with each other.

First law - ΔU = Q - W. Internal energy U = 3/2 nRT for monoatomic 
ideal gas.

Isobaric process (constant pressure): W = p(V_f - V_i).
Isochoric process (constant volume): ΔU = Q = nC_vΔT.
Isothermal process (constant temperature): ΔU = 0, W = Q.
Adiabatic process (no heat transfer): ΔU = -W.

[TOPIC 5.3: HEAT ENGINES]
Thermal efficiency: η = W/Q_H = (Q_H - Q_C)/Q_H.
Carnot engine efficiency: η_C = 1 - T_C/T_H.

Four-stroke engine: intake, compression, explosion, power, valve 
exhaust, exhaust. Diesel engine: intake air only, fuel ignites 
from compression heat.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 6: EFFECTS AND CHARACTERISTICS OF SOUND]
============================================================

[TOPIC 6.1: PROPAGATION OF SOUND]
Speed of sound - Solid: v = √(Y/ρ), Liquid: v = √(B/ρ), Gas: 
v = √(γp/ρ). Faster in solids than liquids than gases.

Intensity level: β(dB) = 10log₁₀(I/I₀). Threshold of hearing: 
10⁻¹² W/m².

[TOPIC 6.2: ECHO]
Echo - Reflection of sound from hard surface. Distance: v = 2d/t. 
Echolocation used by bats and whales.

[TOPIC 6.3: AUDIBILITY]
Human hearing range: 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Infrasound (<20 Hz), 
ultrasound (>20,000 Hz). Ultrasound applications: quality control, 
prenatal testing, dentistry, cleaning, sonar.

[TOPIC 6.4: DOPPLER EFFECT]
Doppler effect - Change in frequency due to relative motion between 
source and observer. f_o = f_s[(v ± v_o)/(v ∓ v_s)].

Applications: red shift (astronomy), radar (velocity measurement), 
Doppler ultrasound (blood flow).

[TOPIC 6.5: PROPERTIES OF SOUND WAVES]
Pitch - Determined by frequency (higher frequency = higher pitch).
Loudness - Determined by amplitude (larger amplitude = louder).
Quality/Timbre - Distinguishes sounds with same loudness and pitch 
from different instruments.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 7: APPLICATIONS OF LIGHT AND OPTICAL INSTRUMENTS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 7.1: CAMERA]
Convex lens focuses light to form real inverted image on film/sensor. 
Aperture controls amount of light. f-number = focal length/diameter 
of aperture. Shutter controls exposure time.

[TOPIC 7.2: HUMAN EYE]
Cornea, aqueous humour, lens, vitreous humour refract light. Iris 
controls pupil size. Retina has rods and cones. Farsightedness 
(hyperopia): images focus behind retina, corrected by convex lens. 
Nearsightedness (myopia): images focus in front of retina, corrected 
by concave lens. Least distance of distinct vision: 25 cm.

[TOPIC 7.3: COMPOUND MICROSCOPE]
Objective lens (short focal length) and eyepiece (short focal length). 
Magnifying power = (v_o/u_o)(25/u_e) for final image at infinity.

[TOPIC 7.4: TELESCOPE]
Astronomical telescope: objective (long focal length), eyepiece 
(short focal length). Magnifying power = f_o/f_e for normal 
adjustment. Reflecting telescope uses concave mirror as objective.

[TOPIC 7.5: LASER]
Laser = Light Amplification by Stimulated Emission of Radiation. 
Essential elements: laser medium, power supply, optical cavity. 
Stimulated emission requires population inversion. Characteristics: 
narrow beam, coherent, monochromatic. Applications: surgery, 
communication, cutting, barcode reading.

[TOPIC 7.6: FIBRE OPTICS]
Based on total internal reflection. Core (higher refractive index) 
and cladding (lower refractive index). Applications: endoscopy, 
communication.

[TOPIC 7.7: SPECTROMETER]
Separates and measures spectral components. Uses prism or grating 
as dispersion element.

[TOPIC 7.8: PHOTOMETRY]
Luminous flux (Φ) - Power of visible light (lumen, lm).
Luminous intensity (I_v) - Flux per unit solid angle (candela, cd).
Luminance - Luminous intensity per unit area (cd/m²).
Illuminance - Flux incident per unit area (lux, lx). 
E_v = I_v/r² (inverse square law).

============================================================
[CHAPTER 8: INTERFERENCE AND DIFFRACTION OF LIGHT]
============================================================

[TOPIC 8.1: HUYGENS' PRINCIPLE]
Every point on wavefront is source of secondary wavelets. New wavefront 
is envelope of wavelets. Wavefront is surface of constant phase.

[TOPIC 8.2: INTERFERENCE OF LIGHT]
Young's double slit experiment: Constructive interference when 
d sinθ = mλ (bright fringes). Destructive interference when 
d sinθ = (m+½)λ (dark fringes). Fringe width: Δy = λD/d.

[TOPIC 8.3: DIFFRACTION OF LIGHT]
Diffraction - Bending of waves around obstacles or through aperture. 
Single slit diffraction: Minima at a sinθ = mλ. Width of central 
maximum = 2λD/a.

[TOPIC 8.4: DIFFRACTION GRATING]
Multiple parallel slits. Condition for maxima: d sinθ = mλ. 
Used to resolve light into spectra.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 9: CAPACITOR AND CAPACITANCE]
============================================================

[TOPIC 9.1: CAPACITORS]
Capacitor - Device storing electrical energy in electric field. 
Capacitance C = Q/V. Unit: farad (F) = C/V. 1 μF = 10⁻⁶ F, 
1 nF = 10⁻⁹ F, 1 pF = 10⁻¹² F.

[TOPIC 9.2: PARALLEL-PLATE CAPACITOR]
C = κε₀A/d. Dielectric constant κ = C/C₀ = V₀/V. Permittivity 
ε = κε₀. Dielectric increases capacitance and maximum operating 
voltage.

[TOPIC 9.3: ENERGY STORED IN CAPACITOR]
W = ½QV = ½CV² = ½Q²/C. Charging: V_c = V₀(1 - e⁻ᵗ/ᴿᶜ). 
Discharging: V_c = V₀e⁻ᵗ/ᴿᴸᶜ. Time constant τ = RC.

[TOPIC 9.4: COMBINATION OF CAPACITORS]
Parallel: C_eq = C₁ + C₂ + C₃ + ...
Series: 1/C_eq = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂ + 1/C₃ + ...

============================================================
[CHAPTER 10: ELECTRICAL ENERGY, POWER AND HEATING EFFECT]
============================================================

[TOPIC 10.1: ELECTROMOTIVE FORCE]
EMF (E) - Energy provided by source per unit charge to move round 
complete circuit. Terminal voltage: V = E - Ir (discharging), 
V = E (open circuit), V = E + Ir (charging). Circuit equation: 
I = E/(R + r).

[TOPIC 10.2: BATTERIES IN SERIES AND PARALLEL]
Series aiding: E_total = E₁ + E₂, r_total = r₁ + r₂.
Series opposing: E_total = E₁ - E₂, r_total = r₁ + r₂.
Parallel (identical batteries): E = E_single, r = r_single/2.

[TOPIC 10.3: ELECTRICAL ENERGY AND POWER]
W = VIt = I²Rt = V²t/R. P = VI = I²R = V²/R. Unit: kWh = 3.60×10⁶ J.

[TOPIC 10.4: JOULE'S LAW]
H = I²Rt/J (J = 4.2 J cal⁻¹). Heat produced directly proportional 
to I², R, and t.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 11: ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 11.1: ALTERNATING CURRENT]
AC: E = E_m sinωt, I = I_m sinωt. ω = 2πf.

[TOPIC 11.2: ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION]
Induced current when magnetic flux through loop changes. 
Magnetic flux: Φ_B = BA cosθ. Magnetic flux density B = μH.

[TOPIC 11.3: FARADAY'S LAW AND LENZ'S LAW]
Faraday's law: E = -N(dΦ_B/dt). Lenz's law: induced emf opposes 
change in magnetic flux.

[TOPIC 11.4: APPLICATIONS]
AC generator: E = NBAω sinωt. Transformer: V_s/V_p = N_s/N_p, 
V_s I_s = V_p I_p (ideal). Step-up: N_s > N_p, V_s > V_p. 
Step-down: N_s < N_p, V_s < V_p.

[TOPIC 11.5: POWER TRANSMISSION]
Power loss = I²R. Transmit at high voltage to reduce current and 
power loss.

[TOPIC 11.6: HOUSE WIRING]
Live wire (high voltage), neutral wire (zero volt), earth wire 
(safety). Circuits: lighting, ring main (power), heating (cooker). 
Fuse protects circuit from overload.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 12: ELECTRONICS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 12.1: ELECTRONIC SYSTEM]
Transducer converts one form of energy to another. Amplifiers increase 
signal power. Oscillators produce AC signals. Logic gates: AND, OR, 
NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR.

[TOPIC 12.2: DIGITAL ELECTRONICS]
Binary system (0 and 1). Logic circuits use Boolean algebra. 
Flip-flops store binary data.

[TOPIC 12.3: BASIC ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATION]
Modulation (AM, FM) combines information signal with carrier wave. 
Demodulation extracts information. Modulation allows efficient 
transmission at higher frequencies.

[CHAPTER 13: MODERN PHYSICS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 13.1: RADIOACTIVITY]
Alpha decay: emits ⁴He nucleus. Beta decay: emits electron. 
Gamma decay: emits high-energy photon. Half-life: time for half of 
radioactive nuclei to decay.

[TOPIC 13.2: NUCLEAR ENERGY]
Nuclear fission: splitting heavy nucleus. Nuclear fusion: combining 
light nuclei. Both release energy from mass defect (E = mc²).

[TOPIC 13.3: WAVE-PARTICLE DUALISM]
Photoelectric effect: light behaves as particles (photons). 
De Broglie wavelength: λ = h/p. Electron diffraction shows wave 
nature of particles.

[TOPIC 13.4: SPECIAL THEORY OF RELATIVITY]
Time dilation: moving clocks run slower. Length contraction: moving 
objects shorten in direction of motion. E = mc² relates mass and 
energy.

SUBJECT: MATHEMATICS
============================================================

[CHAPTER 1: COMPLEX NUMBERS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 1.1: PURE IMAGINARY UNIT]
i² = -1. Complex number z = x + yi = (x,y). Operations on complex 
numbers follow usual rules, but i² = -1.

[TOPIC 1.2: COMPLEX NUMBER OPERATIONS]
Addition: (a+bi)+(c+di) = (a+c)+(b+d)i.
Multiplication: (a+bi)(c+di) = (ac-bd)+(ad+bc)i.
Division: multiply numerator and denominator by conjugate of denominator.
Conjugate: \bar{z} = x - yi.

[TOPIC 1.3: TRIGONOMETRIC FORM]
z = r(cosθ + i sinθ), r = |z| = √(x²+y²), x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ.
Product: z₁z₂ = r₁r₂[cos(θ₁+θ₂) + i sin(θ₁+θ₂)].
Division: z₁/z₂ = (r₁/r₂)[cos(θ₁-θ₂) + i sin(θ₁-θ₂)].
Powers: zⁿ = rⁿ(cos nθ + i sin nθ).

[TOPIC 1.4: ROOTS OF COMPLEX NUMBERS]
n-th roots: z^(1/n) = r^(1/n)[cos((θ+2πk)/n) + i sin((θ+2πk)/n)], 
k = 0, 1, 2, ..., n-1.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 2: MATHEMATICAL INDUCTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 2.1: PRINCIPLE OF MATHEMATICAL INDUCTION]
Step 1: Verify statement true for n = 1 (base case).
Step 2: Assume true for n = k (induction hypothesis).
Step 3: Prove true for n = k + 1 using the assumption.
Conclusion: Statement true for all natural numbers n.

Examples: sum of first n odd numbers = n², sum of first n natural 
numbers = n(n+1)/2.

[CHAPTER 3: ANALYTICAL SOLID GEOMETRY]
============================================================

[TOPIC 3.1: COORDINATES IN SPACE]
Point P(x,y,z). Planes: xy-plane (z=0), yz-plane (x=0), zx-plane 
(y=0). Distance between points: 
d = √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)² + (z₂-z₁)²].

[TOPIC 3.2: LINES]
Directed values: ⟨l,m,n⟩ = ⟨x₂-x₁, y₂-y₁, z₂-z₁⟩.
Parametric equation: (x,y,z) = (x₁+kl, y₁+km, z₁+kn).
Coordinate form: (x-x₁)/l = (y-y₁)/m = (z-z₁)/n.

[TOPIC 3.3: PARALLEL, SKEW, PERPENDICULAR LINES]
Parallel: direction vectors are multiples.
Perpendicular: dot product of direction vectors = 0.
Skew: neither parallel nor intersecting.

[TOPIC 3.4: PLANES]
Equation: ax + by + cz = d. Normal vector: ⟨a,b,c⟩.
Plane through three points: use cross product of two vectors in plane.

[TOPIC 3.5: SPHERES]
Equation: (x-h)² + (y-k)² + (z-l)² = r². Center (h,k,l), radius r.
Tangent plane: line from centre to point of tangency is perpendicular.

[CHAPTER 4: VECTOR ALGEBRA]
============================================================

[TOPIC 4.1: VECTORS IN 3D]
Vector \vec{a} = ⟨a₁,a₂,a₃⟩. Addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication 
component-wise. Magnitude: |\vec{a}| = √(a₁²+a₂²+a₃²).
Unit vector: \hat{a} = \vec{a}/|\vec{a}|.

[TOPIC 4.2: SCALAR PRODUCT]
\vec{a}·\vec{b} = a₁b₁ + a₂b₂ + a₃b₃ = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|cosθ.
Perpendicular: \vec{a}·\vec{b} = 0. Parallel: \vec{a}·\vec{b} = 
±|\vec{a}||\vec{b}|.

[TOPIC 4.3: VECTOR PRODUCT]
\vec{a}×\vec{b} = |\vec{a}||\vec{b}|sinθ \hat{n}. 
Component form: \vec{a}×\vec{b} = ⟨a₂b₃-a₃b₂, a₃b₁-a₁b₃, a₁b₂-a₂b₁⟩.
Area of parallelogram = |\vec{a}×\vec{b}|.
Area of triangle = ½|\vec{a}×\vec{b}|.

[TOPIC 4.4: LINES AND PLANES IN SPACE]
Line: \vec{r} = \vec{a} + t\vec{b}. Plane: \vec{r} = \vec{a} + t₁\vec{b₁} 
+ t₂\vec{b₂}. Normal: \vec{n} = \vec{b₁}×\vec{b₂}. 
Cartesian plane: \vec{r}·\vec{n} = \vec{a}·\vec{n}.

[CHAPTER 5: PERMUTATIONS AND COMBINATIONS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 5.1: COUNTING PRINCIPLES]
Multiplication principle: m ways then n ways = mn ways.
Factorial: n! = n(n-1)(n-2)...1, 0! = 1.

[TOPIC 5.2: PERMUTATIONS]
P(n,r) = n!/(n-r)! = n(n-1)...(n-r+1).
Number of arrangements of n objects with repetitions: n!/(n₁!n₂!...).

[TOPIC 5.3: COMBINATIONS]
C(n,r) = n!/[r!(n-r)!].
Properties: C(n,0)=1, C(n,n)=1, C(n,r)=C(n,n-r).

[TOPIC 5.4: TECHNIQUES]
Sum of subsets: C(n,0)+C(n,1)+...+C(n,n)=2ⁿ.

[CHAPTER 6: CONIC SECTIONS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 6.1: CIRCLES]
Standard form: (x-h)² + (y-k)² = r². Center (h,k), radius r.
General form: x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0.
Center: (-g,-f), radius = √(g²+f²-c).

[TOPIC 6.2: PARABOLAS]
Vertex at (0,0): y² = 4px (opens right), y² = -4px (opens left), 
x² = 4py (opens up), x² = -4py (opens down).
Vertex at (h,k): (y-k)² = 4p(x-h), (x-h)² = 4p(y-k).

[TOPIC 6.3: ROTATION OF AXES]
x = x'cosθ - y'sinθ, y = x'sinθ + y'cosθ.
cot 2θ = (A-C)/B to remove xy term.

[CHAPTER 7: TRIGONOMETRIC FUNCTIONS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 7.1: GRAPHS]
Period: 2π for sin, cos; π for tan, cot.
Amplitude = |a| for y = a sin(bx).
Five key points method for graphing.

[TOPIC 7.2: INVERSE TRIG FUNCTIONS]
sin⁻¹ x restricted domain [-π/2, π/2].
cos⁻¹ x restricted domain [0, π].
tan⁻¹ x restricted domain (-π/2, π/2).

[TOPIC 7.3: DIFFERENTIATION]
d/dx sin x = cos x, d/dx cos x = -sin x, d/dx tan x = sec² x,
d/dx cot x = -csc² x, d/dx sec x = sec x tan x, d/dx csc x = -csc x cot x.

[CHAPTER 8: LOGARITHMIC AND EXPONENTIAL FUNCTIONS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 8.1: LOGARITHMIC FUNCTIONS]
log_b x = y ⇔ bʸ = x. Domain: x > 0, Range: all real.
Natural log: ln x = log_e x.

[TOPIC 8.2: DIFFERENTIATION]
d/dx log_b x = 1/(x ln b), d/dx ln x = 1/x.

[TOPIC 8.3: EXPONENTIAL FUNCTIONS]
y = bˣ. Domain: all real. Range: y > 0.

[TOPIC 8.4: DIFFERENTIATION]
d/dx bˣ = bˣ ln b, d/dx eˣ = eˣ.
d/dx b^{u(x)} = b^{u(x)} ln b · du/dx.

[CHAPTER 9: APPLICATION OF DIFFERENTIATION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 9.1: TANGENT AND NORMAL LINES]
Tangent: y - y₁ = f'(x₁)(x - x₁).
Normal: y - y₁ = -1/f'(x₁)(x - x₁) (if f'(x₁) ≠ 0).

[TOPIC 9.2: MAXIMUM AND MINIMUM]
Critical point: f'(x) = 0 or undefined.
First derivative test: sign change from + to - = local max, - to + = local min.
Second derivative test: f''(x) > 0 = local min, f''(x) < 0 = local max.
Concavity: f''(x) > 0 = concave up, f''(x) < 0 = concave down.
Inflection point: f''(x) changes sign.

[TOPIC 9.3: LINEARIZATION]
L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x-a). Approximates f(x) near x = a.

∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C (n ≠ -1).
∫1/x dx = ln|x| + C.
∫eˣ dx = eˣ + C.
∫cos x dx = sin x + C.

[TOPIC 10.2: SUBSTITUTION METHOD]
∫f(g(x))g'(x) dx = ∫f(u) du where u = g(x).

[TOPIC 10.3: INTEGRATION BY PARTS]
∫u dv = uv - ∫v du.

[TOPIC 10.4: PARTIAL FRACTION METHOD]
Decompose rational function into simpler fractions:
(px+q)/(x²+ax+b) = A/(x-α) + B/(x-β).

∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) - F(a) where F is antiderivative.

SUBJECT: ECONOMICS

[CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION TO ECONOMICS]
============================================================

[TOPIC 1.1: DEFINITION OF ECONOMICS]
Economics is the study of how individuals, firms, and societies 
choose to allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants. 
Scarcity - limited resources relative to unlimited wants.

[TOPIC 1.2: KEY ECONOMIC CONCEPTS]
Opportunity cost - the value of the next best alternative forgone.
Choice - selecting among alternatives due to scarcity.
Trade-offs - all choices involve giving up something.

[TOPIC 1.3: ECONOMIC SYSTEMS]
Market economy - decisions by individuals and firms.
Command economy - decisions by government.
Mixed economy - combination of market and command.

[TOPIC 1.4: PRODUCTION POSSIBILITIES CURVE]
Shows maximum combinations of two goods that can be produced with 
available resources. Points: inside = inefficient, on = efficient, 
outside = unattainable. Shifts = economic growth.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 2: DEMAND AND SUPPLY]
============================================================

[TOPIC 2.1: DEMAND]
Law of demand - as price increases, quantity demanded decreases 
(ceteris paribus). Demand curve slopes downward.

Determinants of demand: income, prices of related goods, tastes, 
expectations, number of buyers.

[TOPIC 2.2: SUPPLY]
Law of supply - as price increases, quantity supplied increases 
(ceteris paribus). Supply curve slopes upward.

Determinants of supply: prices of inputs, technology, expectations, 
number of sellers, taxes and subsidies.

[TOPIC 2.3: MARKET EQUILIBRIUM]
Equilibrium price and quantity where demand equals supply.
Surplus: price above equilibrium, quantity supplied exceeds 
quantity demanded. Shortage: price below equilibrium, quantity 
demanded exceeds quantity supplied.

[TOPIC 2.4: ELASTICITY]
Price elasticity of demand - measures responsiveness of quantity 
demanded to price change.
E_d = (%ΔQd)/(%ΔP).
Elastic: |E_d| > 1, Inelastic: |E_d| < 1, Unit elastic: |E_d| = 1.

Cross elasticity: responsiveness of demand for one good to price 
change of another good. Income elasticity: responsiveness of demand 
to income change.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 3: THEORY OF PRODUCTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 3.1: PRODUCTION FUNCTION]
Shows relationship between inputs and outputs. Q = f(L, K) for 
short-run (capital fixed) and Q = f(L, K) for long-run (all 
inputs variable).

[TOPIC 3.2: SHORT-RUN PRODUCTION]
Total product (TP) - total output.
Average product (AP) = TP/L.
Marginal product (MP) = ΔTP/ΔL.

Law of diminishing marginal returns - as more units of a variable 
input are added to fixed inputs, marginal product eventually 
diminishes.

[TOPIC 3.3: STAGES OF PRODUCTION]
Stage 1: MP increasing, AP increasing (increasing returns).
Stage 2: MP decreasing but positive, AP decreasing (diminishing 
returns) - rational producer operates here.
Stage 3: MP negative (negative returns).

============================================================
[CHAPTER 4: COSTS OF PRODUCTION]
============================================================

[TOPIC 4.1: TYPES OF COSTS]
Explicit costs - actual monetary payments (wages, rent, materials).
Implicit costs - opportunity costs of using owner's resources 
(normal profit, foregone interest).

[TOPIC 4.2: SHORT-RUN COSTS]
Fixed costs (FC) - do not vary with output (rent, insurance).
Variable costs (VC) - vary with output (wages, materials).
Total cost (TC) = FC + VC.
Average fixed cost (AFC) = FC/Q.
Average variable cost (AVC) = VC/Q.
Average total cost (ATC) = TC/Q = AFC + AVC.
Marginal cost (MC) = ΔTC/ΔQ = ΔVC/ΔQ.

Cost curves: AFC downward sloping, AVC U-shaped, ATC U-shaped, 
MC U-shaped. MC intersects ATC and AVC at their minimum points.

============================================================
[CHAPTER 5: MARKET STRUCTURES]
============================================================

[TOPIC 5.1: PERFECT COMPETITION]
Many firms, identical products, free entry and exit, perfect 
information. Price takers. In short-run: P = MR = MC for profit 
maximization. Supernormal profits if P > ATC, losses if P < ATC, 
normal profits if P = ATC. In long-run: firms earn normal profit 
(P = ATC = MC).

[TOPIC 5.2: MONOPOLY]
One firm, unique product, barriers to entry. Price maker. 
Profit maximizing: MR = MC. Price determined by demand curve. 
Can earn supernormal profits in long-run.

Barriers to entry: economies of scale, patents, government licenses, 
control of resources.

[TOPIC 5.3: OLIGOPOLY]
Few firms, strategic interdependence. Products may be homogeneous 
or differentiated. Barriers to entry. Price rigidity (kinked demand 
curve). Cartels: collusion to maximize joint profits.

[TOPIC 5.4: MONOPOLISTIC COMPETITION]
Many firms, differentiated products, free entry and exit. 
Non-price competition (advertising, branding). In short-run: 
can earn supernormal profits. In long-run: normal profits (P = ATC).

============================================================

[TOPIC 6.1: PRODUCTION AND OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT]
Production - creating goods. Operations - processes that transform

[TOPIC 6.2: MARKETING MANAGEMENT]
Marketing - identifying and satisfying customer needs. Marketing

[TOPIC 6.3: FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT]
Financial management - planning, organising, directing, controlling

[TOPIC 6.4: HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT]

[TOPIC 6.5: SMALL BUSINESS MANAGEMENT]

[CHAPTER 7: ECONOMIC GROWTH]
============================================================

[TOPIC 7.1: NOMINAL AND REAL GDP]
Nominal GDP - valued at current prices (includes inflation).
Real GDP - valued at constant prices (adjusted for inflation).
GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP/Real GDP) × 100.

[TOPIC 7.2: ECONOMIC GROWTH RATE]
Growth rate = [(Real GDP₁ - Real GDP₀)/Real GDP₀] × 100.

[TOPIC 7.3: GDP DEFLATOR]
Measures changes in price level of all goods produced domestically.

[TOPIC 7.4: BUSINESS CYCLE]
Patterns of expansion and contraction. Phases: peak, recession, 
trough, recovery.

[TOPIC 7.5: LONG-RUN ECONOMIC GROWTH]
Sources: physical capital, human capital, technological progress, 
natural resources. Capital deepening - increasing capital per worker.

[TOPIC 7.6: MACROECONOMIC EQUILIBRIUM]
Equilibrium output where AD = AS. Short-run: economy can be above 
or below full employment. Long-run: equilibrium at full employment 
output.

[TOPIC 7.7: STABILIZATION POLICIES]
Fiscal policy - government spending and taxation.
Monetary policy - money supply and interest rates (controlled by 
central bank).

[TOPIC 8.1: FOREIGN EXCHANGE]
Exchange rate - price of one currency in terms of another.

[TOPIC 8.2: BALANCE OF PAYMENTS]
Record of all economic transactions between residents of one country

[TOPIC 8.3: BALANCE OF PAYMENTS ADJUSTMENT]
Methods: exchange rate changes, monetary policy, fiscal policy,

[TOPIC 8.4: ECONOMIC INTEGRATION]
